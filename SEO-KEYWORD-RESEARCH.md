## Important data-quality note

The keyword ideas below are prioritized using relevance, search intent, and qualitative competition estimates. Exact monthly search volume and numeric keyword-difficulty scores were **not verified from a live paid keyword database** in this implementation. Do not present the previously mentioned ~11,000 monthly searches as confirmed demand for the QueueTurn brand. Broad category volume, a third-party estimate, and brand searches are different metrics. Validate exact volumes by country in Google Keyword Planner or a keyword tool, then replace qualitative estimates with the measured values.

## Why start with long-tail keywords?

- They describe a specific use case and are closer to a buyer's actual problem.
- They let QueueTurn build topical coverage around small businesses, clinics, salons, restaurants, service centers, and retail.
- They avoid relying exclusively on broad, highly competitive terms such as “queue management software.”
- Each article answers a distinct question and links to relevant product information without claiming unverified product features.

## Published article cluster

The blog currently contains 18 educational articles covering:
- Core concepts: queue management software, virtual queues, digital tokens, digital vs paper queues.
- Industry workflows: clinics, salons, restaurants, retail, service centers.
- Buying decisions: small-business systems, free plans, software checklist, queue vs appointments.
- Operational improvement: wait-time management, reducing waits, and system benefits.

## Technical SEO implemented in this content rollout

- Separate crawlable HTML file for every article under `/blog/<slug>/`.
- Unique page title and meta description, canonical URL, Open Graph tags, and mobile-responsive styles.
- Article and BreadcrumbList JSON-LD on each article; CollectionPage JSON-LD on the blog index.
- Internal links between the homepage, pricing section, blog index, and article pages.
- XML sitemap at `https://www.queueturn.com/sitemap.xml`.
- `robots.txt` references the sitemap.
- `llms.txt` provides a concise site and article index for systems that choose to consume it. It is not a Google ranking requirement.
- Homepage canonical, social URL metadata, and SoftwareApplication JSON-LD.

## Next steps to make this evidence-led

1. In Google Search Console, submit `https://www.queueturn.com/sitemap.xml` and inspect the homepage, blog index, and several article URLs.
2. Use URL Inspection to confirm Google can fetch and index the actual deployed HTML pages.
3. In Google Keyword Planner, choose the target country (start with the United States if that is the acquisition market), export monthly searches and competition, and update this document.
4. After 28–60 days, export Search Console queries and page performance. Prioritize pages with impressions but low CTR, and expand articles that gain relevant impressions.
5. Add first-hand screenshots, original workflow examples, or measured product demonstrations as they become available. Keep product claims aligned with current functionality.
6. Review analytics and conversion events before judging content success; rankings and traffic are not guaranteed by publishing a sitemap or articles.

## Search and AI visibility guidance

Follow Google's people-first content guidance. Helpful content, clear site structure, crawlable HTML, accurate titles, descriptive links, and consistent organization information help search engines understand the site. Do not keyword-stuff pages or publish thin variations merely to target every phrase. AI-search visibility cannot be guaranteed by a special file or schema; make each guide specific, factual, and useful enough to cite.
